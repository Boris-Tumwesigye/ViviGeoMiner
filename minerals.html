Explore the Mineral World

Learn about important minerals, their chemical compositions, physical characteristics, geological occurrences and uses. This library is designed as a learning resource for mineral enthusiasts, students and geological field explorers.

Gold

Au

Appearance

Usually yellow metallic, soft and highly malleable.

Geological occurrence

Found in quartz veins, hydrothermal deposits and alluvial sediments.

Uses

Jewellery, electronics, dentistry and investment.

Identification clues

Gold is very dense, malleable and does not tarnish easily. Pyrite can look similar but is brittle.

Copper

Cu

Appearance

Native copper is reddish-brown with a metallic lustre.

Geological occurrence

Occurs in hydrothermal veins and several types of copper ore deposits.

Uses

Electrical wiring, plumbing, electronics and industrial equipment.

Identification clues

Native copper is malleable. Copper minerals may have green, blue, red or brassy colours.

Iron Ore

Fe₂O₃ / Fe₃O₄

Appearance

Hematite is often reddish-brown to steel-grey. Magnetite is commonly black.

Geological occurrence

Found in banded iron formations, sedimentary deposits and other geological settings.

Uses

Steel production, construction, machinery and manufacturing.

Identification clues

Magnetite is strongly magnetic. Hematite commonly leaves a reddish-brown streak.

Lithium

Li

Appearance

Lithium occurs in minerals such as spodumene and lepidolite, not usually as native metal.

Geological occurrence

Commonly associated with certain granitic pegmatites and some salt-lake brines.

Uses

Rechargeable batteries, ceramics, glass and specialised industrial products.

Identification clues

Spodumene may occur as elongated crystals. Laboratory analysis is needed to confirm lithium content.

Limestone

CaCO₃

Appearance

Often white, cream, grey or brown, and may contain visible fossils.

Geological occurrence

Usually forms in sedimentary environments, including marine settings.

Uses

Cement, construction, agriculture and industrial processing.

Identification clues

Calcite-rich limestone commonly reacts with dilute hydrochloric acid by producing bubbles.

Zinc

Zn

Appearance

Zinc is commonly extracted from sphalerite, which can be brown, black, yellow or reddish.

Geological occurrence

Often occurs in hydrothermal veins and lead-zinc deposits.

Uses

Galvanising steel, batteries, alloys and industrial manufacturing.

Identification clues

Sphalerite can have a resinous lustre and distinctive cleavage. Colour alone cannot confirm zinc.

Silver

Ag

Appearance

Native silver is usually bright white metallic but can tarnish to grey or black.

Geological occurrence

Occurs in hydrothermal veins and may be associated with lead, copper and gold ores.

Uses

Electronics, solar technology, jewellery and specialised industrial applications.

Identification clues

Silver is highly malleable. Other white metallic minerals may look similar.

Lead

Pb

Appearance

Lead is commonly obtained from galena, a heavy, metallic, lead-grey mineral.

Geological occurrence

Often found in hydrothermal veins and lead-zinc deposits.

Uses

Lead-acid batteries and specialised industrial applications.

Identification clues

Galena is unusually dense and commonly forms cubic crystals.

Important Scientific and Safety Note

Appearance alone cannot reliably identify a mineral or establish its economic value. Confirm suspected minerals through appropriate geological testing and qualified laboratory analysis.

Never taste, inhale, grind or handle unknown mineral samples carelessly. Some minerals and dusts can be toxic.
